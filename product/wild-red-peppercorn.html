Wild Red Peppercorn

Wild-harvested and left to ripen fully — bright, floral heat with none of black pepper's sharp bite.

What It Is

The same climbing vine as black pepper, but picked fully ripe and wild-harvested rather than farmed for yield. Ripening on the vine gives it a deep red colour, natural sweetness, and a bright citrus-like top note alongside pepper's familiar warmth.

How to Use

Treat it as a finishing spice, not a background one. Crack it coarsely with a mortar and pestle or grinder right before serving — steak, roasted vegetables, finishing butters, or even fruit and chocolate desserts.

How to Store

Keep whole peppercorns in an airtight jar away from light and heat. Grind just before use — pre-ground pepper loses its aromatic oils within weeks, whole corns hold their character for up to two years.

At a Glance
  • Wild-harvested, not farmed
  • Same vine as black pepper
  • Cracked, not ground fine
  • Citrus-bright heat

How Much to Use

Steak / pan sauce
2 servings
1 tsp cracked
Roasted vegetables
1 tray
½ tsp cracked
Finishing butter
100 g butter
1 tsp cracked
Fruit / chocolate dessert
4 servings
pinch, cracked
Spice rub
1 batch
1 tbsp cracked
Table finishing
to taste
a few turns
Tip for Home Cooks

Add it at the end of cooking, not the start — heat burns off the delicate citrus notes that set wild red peppercorn apart from ordinary black pepper.
